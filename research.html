Research

Dr. Ashia Wilson runs the Optimization, Safety, and Evaluation (OSE) Lab.

Our research studies how to measure, improve, and reason about machine learning systems. Current work synthesizes four connected directions: evaluation methods that better reflect real-world decision costs and capabilities; algorithmic pluralism and homogenization in deployed systems; privacy-preserving learning and machine unlearning; and core optimization and sampling methods for modern ML.

Evaluation

We develop frameworks for evaluating AI systems in ways that align with downstream use, including clinical priorities, benchmark validity, scoring rules, and harms assessment.

Algorithmic pluralism and homogenization

We study how algorithmic systems can concentrate decisions, reduce diversity, or reshape competition, and how pluralistic or randomized approaches can support fairer outcomes.

Privacy and unlearning

We design methods for private statistical learning and for removing data, concepts, or behaviors from trained models while understanding when unlearning can fail.

Optimization and sampling

We develop optimization and sampling algorithms that improve the reliability and efficiency of machine learning pipelines.
